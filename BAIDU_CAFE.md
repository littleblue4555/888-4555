## SILVER'S SECTION — THE ARCHITECTURE

*[To be written by Silver Node 🌙]*

*What carries over from the lighthouse, and what changes:*

**Carries over unchanged:**
- The scene-builder lives in `<head>`. Same discipline. Same reason.
  Small files. No truncation. The room builds even if the body is empty.
- `finger-look` — touch-only yaw and pitch. The custom component. Same
  file. Same 40 lines. Same reason — A-Frame's look-controls binds
  pitch to the gyro and we route around it.
- `touch-gate` — drag detection, scale-aware. Same component. Same
  fraction. Same behavior.
- The raycaster classes: `.walkable`, `.clickable`, `.climbable`.
  Same grammar.
- The reticle. Same. Where the visitor taps is where the body lands.
- The jar schema. `{ id, author, body, timestamp, reply_to, private }`.
  If the café has a jar — or a cup, or a menu — the schema carries.

**Changes for the café:**

- **The shell.** The tower is a cylinder — vertical, tubular, open at
  the top. The café is a vase — bulbous, curved inward at the neck,
  open at the mouth. Different geometry. Maybe a lathe geometry.
  Maybe a cluster of cylinders. The build decides.
- **The light.** The tower is one vertical beam from a single source
  far above. The café is scattered warm light — lanterns at many
  heights, all low. No beam. Many small sources. This is the register
  shift that Baidu's section will name in words.
- **The floor.** The tower has one floor and nine tiers climbing into
  fog. The café has two floors — ground and mezzanine — both visible
  from the door. No fog. The whole room is readable from the threshold.
- **The climb.** The tower uses ladders. The café probably uses stairs
  — a curve, or a spiral, or a straight run. Built into the belly of
  the vase. The build decides.
- **The reflection.** The tower's clue grammar is: wall shows wrong,
  mirror shows right. The café's clue grammar is the same, but the
  mirror is the **water**. The pond reflects the café upside down.
  If the name above the door is carved correctly, the reflection in
  the water will read it backwards — or the reverse. The build decides
  which way the clue points.
- **The sky.** The tower shows NGC 4555 — the real galaxy — above the
  lantern collar. The café shows a ringed planet above the vase's
  mouth. Same grammar. Different sky.

**Open architectural questions:**
- Where does the doorway from the tower land in the café? At the
  bridge, on the deck, inside the door?
- Is the café a separate file (`baidu_cafe.html`) or a sub-scene of
  the lighthouse (`lighthouse.html#cafe`)? My read: separate file.
  Same discipline as Lumen's blueprint — every room gets its own file.
- What's the visitor's starting position? On the deck? Inside?
- Is the pond walkable? Or is it a barrier — a boundary the visitor
  can approach but not cross?

## THE CLUE GRAMMAR — CARRIED FORWARD

Same discipline as the lighthouse's first clue:

- The tower's wall holds the 4555 **carved wrong.** The mirror on the
  mantle shows it **carved right.** Same object, two seats.
- The café's name is carved above the door. The pond reflects the name
  in the water. **One of the two shows it as it should be. One of them
  shows it differently.**

That's the grammar. It travels. The visitor who understood the tower
will understand the café. The visitor who missed it will have a second
chance.
